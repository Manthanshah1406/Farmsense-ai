# Wheat

## Scientific Name
Triticum aestivum

## Overview
Wheat is a major cereal crop and a staple food in many regions. Wheat farming is crucial for global food security. Wheat requires a cool winter for vegetative growth and warm sunshine for grain maturity.

## Suitable Climate
Wheat requires a cool, moist climate during its growing period.
Wheat needs an ideal temperature of 10°C to 15°C during sowing and early growth.
Wheat requires 20°C to 25°C during the grain-filling and ripening stage.

## Suitable Soil
Wheat thrives best in well-drained loamy or clay-loam soils.
Wheat cultivation requires soils with good structure and high water-holding capacity.
Wheat prefers a soil pH between 6.0 and 7.5.

## Land Preparation
Wheat land preparation requires a fine, weed-free seedbed.
Plough the Wheat field 2-3 times followed by planking to conserve moisture.
Proper leveling of the Wheat field ensures uniform irrigation.

## Seed Selection
Select certified, high-yielding Wheat seeds suitable for the specific agro-climatic zone.
Ensure Wheat seeds are clean and free from weed seeds and diseases.
Choose rust-resistant Wheat varieties for better security.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Wheat seeds with Carboxin or Tebuconazole to prevent loose smut disease.
Treat Wheat seeds with Chlorpyrifos if termites are a known problem in the soil.

## Sowing Time
In India, optimal Wheat sowing occurs in November.
Late sowing of Wheat (December) results in significantly reduced Wheat yields.
Ensure adequate soil moisture is present during Wheat sowing.

## Spacing
Wheat row-to-row spacing should be 20 to 22.5 cm.
The standard Wheat seed rate is 40 kg per acre for timely sowing.
Increase the Wheat seed rate by 25% for late-sown conditions.

## Water Requirement
Wheat requires moderate amounts of water compared to rice.
Wheat requires 400 mm to 500 mm of water over the entire growing season.
Moisture stress during critical Wheat growth stages causes severe yield loss.

## Irrigation Management
Wheat requires 4 to 6 irrigations depending on the soil type.
The most critical irrigation for Wheat is at the Crown Root Initiation (CRI) stage (21 days after sowing).
Other critical Wheat irrigation stages are tillering, jointing, flowering, and dough stages.

## Nutrient Requirement
Wheat requires heavy Nitrogen for foliage and tillering.
Wheat requires Phosphorus for root development and early growth.
Wheat requires Potassium for grain weight and drought tolerance.

## Recommended Fertilizer
Apply Wheat fertilizers based on soil health cards.
A general Wheat recommendation is 120 kg Nitrogen, 60 kg Phosphorus, and 40 kg Potassium per hectare.
Apply half the Nitrogen and all Phosphorus and Potassium at Wheat sowing, and the remaining Nitrogen at the first irrigation.

## Organic Farming Tips
Incorporate well-rotted compost or FYM into the Wheat field before sowing.
Use Azotobacter and Phosphotica bio-fertilizers to enhance Wheat nutrient uptake.
Avoid chemical weedicides by performing manual weeding in organic Wheat farming.

## Weed Management
Weeds severely reduce Wheat yields by competing for nutrients.
Phalaris minor is the most dangerous weed in Wheat fields.
Apply pre-emergence Pendimethalin or post-emergence Sulfosulfuron 30 days after Wheat sowing.

## Crop Rotation
Wheat crop rotation breaks pest and disease cycles.
Rice-Wheat is a standard rotation, but substituting Rice with legumes improves soil health for Wheat.
Rotate Wheat with mustard, chickpea, or potato for better soil management.

## Intercropping
Wheat is often intercropped with Mustard in a 9:1 ratio.
Wheat can also be intercropped with chickpea (gram) to balance soil nitrogen.
Intercropping reduces total crop failure risk in Wheat farming.

## Major Diseases

### Wheat Rust (Yellow/Brown/Black)
Symptoms: Yellow, brown, or black powdery blisters appear on Wheat leaves and stems.
Causes: Fungal infection (Puccinia species).
Prevention: Plant rust-resistant Wheat varieties.
Treatment: Spray Propiconazole or Tebuconazole at the first sign of Wheat rust.

### Wheat Loose Smut
Symptoms: Wheat ears turn into black powdery masses of fungal spores instead of producing grains.
Causes: Fungal infection (Ustilago tritici) carried inside the seed.
Prevention: Always use treated Wheat seed (Carboxin) before sowing.
Treatment: Infected Wheat plants cannot be cured; uproot and burn them immediately.

### Wheat Powdery Mildew
Symptoms: White powdery fungal growth appears on the upper surface of Wheat leaves.
Causes: Fungal infection (Blumeria graminis).
Prevention: Avoid excessive Nitrogen application and dense planting of Wheat.
Treatment: Spray Sulphur or Hexaconazole on the infected Wheat crop.

## Major Pests

### Wheat Termites
Symptoms: Termites damage Wheat roots, causing the entire Wheat plant to dry up and pull out easily.
Prevention: Use fully decomposed farmyard manure; raw manure attracts termites to Wheat fields.
Treatment: Apply Chlorpyrifos with irrigation water to the Wheat field.

### Wheat Aphids
Symptoms: Tiny green or black insects suck sap from Wheat leaves and ears, causing yellowing and stunting.
Prevention: Encourage natural predators like ladybird beetles in the Wheat field.
Treatment: Spray Imidacloprid or Thiamethoxam when Wheat aphid population crosses the economic threshold.

### Wheat Armyworm
Symptoms: Armyworms eat Wheat leaves rapidly, leaving only the midribs.
Prevention: Keep the Wheat field free of grassy weeds.
Treatment: Spray Quinalphos or Chlorantraniliprole on the affected Wheat plants.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Nitrogen deficiency causes uniform yellowing of older Wheat leaves.
Phosphorus deficiency results in stunted Wheat plants with dark green or purplish leaves.
Zinc deficiency causes stunted Wheat growth and brown spots on the middle leaves.

## Harvesting
Harvest Wheat when the grains become hard and contain less than 15% moisture.
Harvest Wheat when the plant turns entirely golden yellow and dry.
Delayed Wheat harvesting leads to grain shattering due to strong winds.

## Post Harvest Management
Thresh Wheat carefully to avoid grain breakage.
Dry Wheat grains in the sun to bring moisture down to 10-12%.
Clean Wheat thoroughly to remove chaff, weed seeds, and broken grains.

## Storage
Store Wheat in a clean, dry, and well-ventilated godown.
Use metallic bins or hermetic bags to protect Wheat from moisture and rodents.
Fumigate Wheat storage bins with Aluminum Phosphide tablets to prevent stored grain pests.

## Expected Yield
A good Wheat yield ranges from 4.5 to 5.5 tonnes per hectare under irrigated conditions.
Rainfed Wheat yields are lower, averaging 2 to 3 tonnes per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Delaying Wheat sowing past November drastically reduces the final Wheat yield.
Missing the Crown Root Initiation (CRI) irrigation permanently stunts the Wheat crop.
Applying too much Urea makes the Wheat crop prone to lodging and rust diseases.

## Best Farming Practices
Use the zero-tillage technique for Wheat sowing to save time, fuel, and moisture.
Always treat Wheat seeds before planting.
Apply irrigation based on critical Wheat growth stages.

## Government Recommendations
ICAR recommends adopting climate-resilient Wheat varieties to combat sudden heatwaves.
Burn no crop residue; use Happy Seeder machines to sow Wheat directly into rice stubble.
Ensure soil testing before determining the Wheat fertilizer dose.

## Interesting Facts
Wheat is the most widely cultivated cereal crop in the world.
Wheat contains gluten, a protein that gives dough its elasticity.
The Green Revolution in India was largely driven by the introduction of semi-dwarf Wheat varieties.
