# Coffee

## Scientific Name
Coffea arabica / Coffea canephora (Robusta)

## Overview
Coffee is a major commercial plantation crop cultivated primarily in hilly terrains. Coffee farming requires a specific microclimate, shade management, and meticulous post-harvest processing. Coffee is an evergreen perennial crop.

## Suitable Climate
Coffee requires a cool to warm tropical climate.
Arabica Coffee prefers temperatures between 15°C and 25°C.
Robusta Coffee thrives in warmer temperatures between 20°C and 30°C.
Coffee requires a well-distributed rainfall of 1500 mm to 2000 mm.

## Suitable Soil
Coffee grows best in deep, friable, porous, and well-drained soils.
Forest soils rich in organic matter are ideal for Coffee cultivation.
Coffee requires slightly acidic soil with a pH between 5.5 and 6.5.
Coffee cannot tolerate water stagnation.

## Land Preparation
Coffee land preparation in hilly areas involves terracing and contour planting to prevent soil erosion.
Clear the forest undergrowth but retain large shade trees for the Coffee estate.
Dig pits measuring 45x45x45 cm and leave them open to weather before planting Coffee.

## Seed Selection
Select high-yielding, disease-resistant Coffee seeds from certified coffee boards.
Choose Arabica Coffee for higher elevations and better quality.
Choose Robusta Coffee for lower elevations and disease resistance.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Coffee seeds with Azospirillum and Phosphobacteria to enhance root growth.
Treat Coffee seeds with a fungicide like Carbendazim to prevent damping-off disease in the nursery.

## Sowing Time
Coffee seeds are sown in nursery beds between January and March.
Coffee seedlings are transplanted to the main field during the monsoon season (June to September).

## Spacing
For Arabica Coffee, maintain a spacing of 1.5 meters by 1.5 meters to 2 meters by 2 meters.
For Robusta Coffee, maintain a wider spacing of 3 meters by 3 meters.

## Water Requirement
Coffee requires abundant water but absolutely no waterlogging.
Coffee needs 1500 mm to 2000 mm of annual rainfall.
Blossom showers (rain during March-April) are extremely critical for Coffee flowering and fruit set.

## Irrigation Management
If natural blossom showers fail, provide sprinkler irrigation to the Coffee estate to induce flowering.
Provide backing irrigation 20 days after the blossom shower to ensure Coffee berry development.
Maintain soil moisture in the Coffee estate during the dry summer months.

## Nutrient Requirement
Coffee requires Nitrogen for vegetative growth and branch development.
Coffee requires Phosphorus for root establishment and early fruit set.
Coffee requires high Potassium for berry development and quality.

## Recommended Fertilizer
A general fertilizer recommendation for bearing Arabica Coffee is 120 kg Nitrogen, 90 kg Phosphorus, and 120 kg Potassium per hectare per year.
Apply Coffee fertilizers in 3 to 4 split doses (Pre-blossom, Post-blossom, Mid-monsoon, Post-monsoon).
Apply lime or dolomite to the Coffee soil once every two years to neutralize soil acidity.

## Organic Farming Tips
Recycle Coffee pulp and husk back into the estate as compost.
Use vermicompost and neem cake heavily in organic Coffee farming.
Maintain diverse shade trees to naturally enhance leaf litter and organic matter in the Coffee estate.

## Weed Management
Weeds compete with Coffee bushes for nutrients, especially during the monsoon.
Slash weeds manually in the Coffee estate; avoid deep digging which damages shallow Coffee roots.
Apply contact herbicides (e.g., Paraquat) carefully, avoiding the Coffee foliage.

## Crop Rotation
As a perennial plantation crop, Coffee is not rotated.
Instead, old or diseased Coffee blocks are uprooted, the soil is rested, and replanting is done.

## Intercropping
In young Coffee estates, intercrop with orange, black pepper, or cardamom to maximize income.
Silver Oak, Erythrina, and Jackfruit are planted as shade trees alongside Coffee.
Never intercrop Coffee with root-knot nematode susceptible crops.

## Major Diseases

### Coffee Leaf Rust
Symptoms: Orange to yellow powdery spots appear on the underside of Coffee leaves, causing severe defoliation.
Causes: Fungal infection (Hemileia vastatrix).
Prevention: Plant rust-resistant Arabica Coffee varieties. Maintain optimal shade.
Treatment: Spray Bordeaux mixture (1%) or Hexaconazole on the Coffee bushes before the monsoon.

### Coffee Black Rot
Symptoms: Coffee leaves and developing berries turn black, rot, and hang from the branches attached by fungal threads.
Causes: Fungal infection (Koleroga noxia) thriving in heavy monsoon fogs.
Prevention: Prune Coffee bushes and shade trees to improve ventilation and sunlight penetration.
Treatment: Spray Bordeaux mixture (1%) on the affected Coffee bushes during a break in the monsoon.

### Coffee Berry Blight
Symptoms: Brown water-soaked lesions appear on green Coffee berries, causing them to drop prematurely.
Causes: Fungal infection (Colletotrichum gloeosporioides).
Prevention: Collect and destroy diseased Coffee berries.
Treatment: Spray Propiconazole on the Coffee crop.

## Major Pests

### Coffee White Stem Borer
Symptoms: Grubs tunnel into the main stem and thick roots of Arabica Coffee, causing yellowing of leaves and death of the plant.
Prevention: Maintain proper shade, as this pest thrives in sun-exposed Coffee plants. Trace and uproot heavily infested Coffee bushes.
Treatment: Wrap the main Coffee stem with Chlorpyrifos-soaked strips or swab the stem with Lindane.

### Coffee Berry Borer
Symptoms: Tiny black beetles bore into the tip of the Coffee berry and breed inside the seed, destroying the coffee bean.
Prevention: Harvest all Coffee berries timely. Practice strict field hygiene by collecting fallen berries.
Treatment: Set up Broca traps (ethanol-methanol traps) in the Coffee estate. Spray Beauveria bassiana (a biological fungus).

### Coffee Mealybug
Symptoms: White, cottony masses cover the tender shoots, flower clusters, and roots of the Coffee plant, causing wilting.
Prevention: Control ants in the Coffee estate, as ants protect and spread mealybugs.
Treatment: Spray Dimethoate or apply systemic insecticides to the Coffee roots.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Nitrogen deficiency causes general yellowing of the entire Coffee bush.
Zinc deficiency causes small, narrow, crinkled leaves (rosette effect) at the tips of Coffee branches.
Magnesium deficiency causes yellowing between the veins of older Coffee leaves while the veins remain green.

## Harvesting
Coffee harvesting is done by hand-picking only ripe, red berries (Selective picking).
Strip picking is sometimes used for Robusta Coffee where all berries are stripped at once.
Harvesting Coffee requires multiple rounds of picking as berries ripen unevenly.

## Post Harvest Management
Coffee is processed using either the Wet method (washed coffee) or the Dry method (cherry coffee).
In the Wet method, Coffee berries are pulped, fermented, washed, and dried to produce parchment coffee.
Dry the Coffee beans uniformly on clean drying yards until moisture drops to 10-11%.

## Storage
Store dried Coffee beans in clean gunny bags placed on wooden pallets.
Keep the Coffee storehouse well-ventilated and strictly free from moisture and strong odors.
Never store Coffee alongside spices, fertilizers, or chemicals, as Coffee absorbs odors rapidly.

## Expected Yield
A well-managed Arabica Coffee estate yields 800 kg to 1200 kg of clean coffee per hectare.
Robusta Coffee yields 1000 kg to 1500 kg per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Over-exposing Arabica Coffee to direct sunlight increases White Stem Borer attacks.
Failing to provide sprinkler irrigation when blossom showers fail results in zero Coffee crop for the year.
Storing wet Coffee beans causes mold growth, completely ruining the coffee cup quality.

## Best Farming Practices
Practice regular pruning to remove dead, diseased, and unproductive Coffee branches.
Regulate shade tree canopies twice a year to ensure 50% filtered sunlight reaches the Coffee bushes.
Maintain a strict nutrient schedule based on periodic leaf and soil analysis of the Coffee estate.

## Government Recommendations
The Coffee Board of India recommends installing weather stations in Coffee estates for precision farming.
Use eco-friendly pulping machines to reduce water pollution from Coffee processing.
Obtain Rainforest Alliance or Organic certification for Coffee to secure premium export prices.

## Interesting Facts
Coffee is the second most traded commodity in the world after crude oil.
Robusta Coffee has twice the caffeine content of Arabica Coffee.
India is unique because almost all its Coffee is grown under a canopy of natural shade trees.
