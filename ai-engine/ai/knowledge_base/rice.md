# Rice

## Scientific Name
Oryza sativa

## Overview
Rice is a staple food crop for a large part of the world's population. Rice farming is a primary agricultural activity in many Asian countries. Rice grows best in warm, humid climates and requires significant amounts of water.

## Suitable Climate
Rice requires a hot and humid climate. 
Rice needs an average temperature of 21°C to 37°C throughout the life cycle. 
Rice growth is severely affected below 15°C. 
Rice requires high humidity and prolonged sunshine.

## Suitable Soil
Rice grows optimally in heavy soils with high water retention capacity.
Clay or clay-loam soils are ideal for Rice cultivation.
Rice requires a soil pH between 5.5 and 6.5.

## Land Preparation
Rice land preparation begins with ploughing the field to a depth of 15-20 cm.
Rice fields must be leveled to ensure uniform standing water.
Rice puddling (ploughing in standing water) is essential to reduce water percolation.

## Seed Selection
Select healthy, disease-free Rice seeds from certified sources.
Choose Rice varieties suited for the local climate and water availability.
High-yielding Rice varieties (HYV) are recommended for better productivity.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Rice seeds with a fungicide (e.g., Carbendazim) to prevent seed-borne diseases.
Soak Rice seeds in saltwater to separate lightweight, damaged seeds which float to the top.

## Sowing Time
In India, Kharif Rice is sown in June-July.
Rabi Rice is sown in November-December.
Summer Rice is sown in March-April.

## Spacing
For transplanted Rice, maintain a spacing of 20 cm between rows and 15 cm between plants.
Direct-seeded Rice requires a seed rate of 20-25 kg per acre.

## Water Requirement
Rice is a highly water-intensive crop.
Rice requires between 1200 mm to 1500 mm of water per season.
Continuous standing water is needed during the vegetative phase of Rice.

## Irrigation Management
Keep 2-5 cm of standing water in the Rice field during the early stages.
Drain the Rice field before applying fertilizers.
Drain all water from the Rice field 10-15 days before Rice harvesting to dry the soil.

## Nutrient Requirement
Rice requires high amounts of Nitrogen for vegetative growth.
Rice requires Phosphorus for root development.
Rice requires Potassium for grain filling and disease resistance.

## Recommended Fertilizer
Apply Nitrogen (Urea), Phosphorus (DAP), and Potassium (MOP) based on soil testing.
A general Rice fertilizer recommendation is 100 kg Nitrogen, 50 kg Phosphorus, and 50 kg Potassium per hectare.
Apply Zinc Sulphate if Rice leaves show zinc deficiency (khaira disease).

## Organic Farming Tips
Use farmyard manure (FYM) or compost during Rice land preparation.
Grow green manure crops like Dhaincha or Sunhemp before planting Rice.
Use Azolla and Blue-Green Algae as bio-fertilizers in Rice fields.

## Weed Management
Weeds compete with Rice for nutrients and water.
Apply pre-emergence herbicides (e.g., Pendimethalin) within 3 days of Rice sowing.
Perform manual weeding 20 and 40 days after transplanting Rice.

## Crop Rotation
Rice crop rotation improves soil health.
Rotate Rice with legumes like chickpea, moong, or urad to fix atmospheric nitrogen.
Rice-Wheat rotation is a common and highly productive cropping system.

## Intercropping
Intercropping is not common in flooded Rice fields.
In upland Rice, intercropping with pigeon pea or soybean is possible.

## Major Diseases

### Rice Blast
Symptoms: Spindle-shaped spots with grey centers and brown margins appear on Rice leaves.
Causes: Fungal infection (Magnaporthe oryzae).
Prevention: Avoid excessive Nitrogen fertilizer in Rice. Use resistant Rice varieties.
Treatment: Spray Tricyclazole or Carbendazim on the infected Rice crop.

### Rice Bacterial Leaf Blight
Symptoms: Yellow to white lesions begin at the leaf tips and spread downwards on the Rice leaves.
Causes: Bacterial infection (Xanthomonas oryzae).
Prevention: Soak Rice seeds in Agrimycin. Ensure proper field drainage.
Treatment: Spray Streptocycline mixed with Copper Oxychloride on the Rice crop.

### Rice Sheath Blight
Symptoms: Oval or irregular greenish-grey spots appear on the Rice leaf sheaths near the water level.
Causes: Fungal infection (Rhizoctonia solani).
Prevention: Maintain proper spacing between Rice plants to reduce humidity.
Treatment: Spray Hexaconazole or Propiconazole on the infected Rice crop.

## Major Pests

### Rice Stem Borer
Symptoms: Dead heart in young Rice plants and white earheads (empty grains) in mature Rice plants.
Prevention: Destroy Rice stubbles after harvest. Avoid late planting of Rice.
Treatment: Apply Cartap Hydrochloride or Chlorantraniliprole granules to the Rice field.

### Rice Brown Plant Hopper (BPH)
Symptoms: BPH sucks sap from the base of Rice plants, causing "hopper burn" where circular patches of Rice plants dry and lodge.
Prevention: Maintain alternate wetting and drying in the Rice field. Avoid excessive Nitrogen.
Treatment: Spray Pymetrozine or Imidacloprid at the base of the Rice plants.

### Rice Leaf Folder
Symptoms: The pest folds Rice leaves longitudinally and feeds on the green tissue, creating white streaks.
Prevention: Keep the Rice field weed-free.
Treatment: Spray Quinalphos or Chlorpyrifos on the affected Rice crop.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Nitrogen deficiency causes uniform yellowing of older Rice leaves.
Zinc deficiency causes brown/bronze spots on lower Rice leaves (Khaira disease).
Iron deficiency causes yellowing of new Rice leaves while veins remain green.

## Harvesting
Harvest Rice when 80% of the panicles turn golden yellow.
Harvest Rice when the grain moisture is around 20%.
Delaying Rice harvesting leads to grain shattering and loss.

## Post Harvest Management
Thresh Rice immediately after harvesting.
Clean the Rice grains to remove chaff and impurities.
Dry the Rice grains thoroughly before storage.

## Storage
Store Rice grains at a moisture level below 14%.
Use gunny bags or hermetic bags for Rice storage.
Fumigate the Rice storage area to prevent grain weevils and rodents.

## Expected Yield
A good Rice yield is between 4 to 6 tonnes per hectare depending on the variety and management.
Hybrid Rice varieties can yield up to 8 tonnes per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Applying too much Urea (Nitrogen) increases Rice susceptibility to pests and diseases.
Keeping Rice fields continuously flooded without drying periods causes root rot.
Ignoring Rice seed treatment leads to early fungal infections.

## Best Farming Practices
Always perform a soil test before applying Rice fertilizers.
Adopt the System of Rice Intensification (SRI) for higher Rice yields with less water.
Practice alternate wetting and drying (AWD) in Rice fields to save water.

## Government Recommendations
The government recommends using drought-tolerant Rice varieties in rainfed regions.
ICAR recommends integrated pest management (IPM) to reduce chemical use in Rice farming.
Provide minimum support price (MSP) registered Rice varieties to ensure profitability.

## Interesting Facts
Rice provides more than one-fifth of the calories consumed worldwide by humans.
The System of Rice Intensification (SRI) was developed in Madagascar to improve Rice yields.
Basmati Rice is famous globally for its unique aroma and long grains.
