# Groundnut

## Scientific Name
Arachis hypogaea

## Overview
Groundnut (Peanut) is a major oilseed and food crop. Groundnut farming is highly popular in tropical and subtropical regions. Groundnut is a legume that enriches the soil by fixing atmospheric nitrogen.

## Suitable Climate
Groundnut requires a warm and long growing season.
The ideal temperature for Groundnut vegetative growth is 27°C to 30°C.
Groundnut requires abundant sunshine and a frost-free period of at least 100 days.
Groundnut requires 500 mm to 700 mm of well-distributed rainfall.

## Suitable Soil
Groundnut thrives in well-drained, loose, friable sandy-loam or loamy soils.
Loose soil is strictly required for Groundnut pegs (flower stalks) to easily penetrate the soil and develop pods.
Groundnut prefers a soil pH between 6.0 and 7.5.
Groundnut is highly susceptible to waterlogging and soil salinity.

## Land Preparation
Groundnut requires a thoroughly pulverized and weed-free seedbed.
Plough the Groundnut field deeply, followed by harrowing to loosen the topsoil.
Level the Groundnut field perfectly to prevent water stagnation.

## Seed Selection
Select high-yielding, disease-resistant Groundnut pods for seed extraction.
Extract Groundnut seeds manually just before sowing to maintain high viability.
Ensure Groundnut seeds have intact seed coats; damaged seeds will not germinate.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Groundnut seeds with Trichoderma viride to prevent collar rot and stem rot.
Inoculate Groundnut seeds with Rhizobium bio-fertilizer to enhance nitrogen fixation.

## Sowing Time
Kharif Groundnut is sown in June-July with the onset of the monsoon.
Rabi/Summer Groundnut is sown in January-February under assured irrigation.

## Spacing
For bunch-type Groundnut, maintain a spacing of 30 cm by 10 cm.
For spreading-type Groundnut, maintain a spacing of 45 cm by 15 cm.
The Groundnut seed rate is typically 80 kg to 100 kg of kernels per hectare.

## Water Requirement
Groundnut requires moderate water.
Moisture stress during Groundnut pegging and pod development severely reduces yield.
However, excessive water causes Groundnut pods to rot in the soil.

## Irrigation Management
Provide light irrigation to Groundnut just before sowing if soil moisture is low.
The most critical Groundnut irrigation stages are flowering, pegging, and pod formation.
Stop irrigating Groundnut 15 days before harvest to allow the pods to mature and dry.

## Nutrient Requirement
Groundnut fixes its own Nitrogen but requires a small starter dose.
Groundnut requires heavy Phosphorus for robust root and pod development.
Groundnut uniquely requires high Calcium and Sulphur for proper pod filling and oil synthesis.

## Recommended Fertilizer
A general Groundnut fertilizer recommendation is 20 kg Nitrogen, 50 kg Phosphorus, and 40 kg Potassium per hectare.
Apply Gypsum (Calcium Sulphate) at 250 kg per hectare at the Groundnut pegging stage to prevent empty pods (pops).

## Organic Farming Tips
Apply well-decomposed farmyard manure during Groundnut land preparation.
Use Phosphorus Solubilizing Bacteria (PSB) alongside Rhizobium in the Groundnut field.
Use Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE) to organically manage Groundnut pests.

## Weed Management
Groundnut is a poor competitor with weeds during the first 30 to 45 days.
Apply pre-emergence Pendimethalin within two days of Groundnut sowing.
Perform hand weeding 20 and 40 days after Groundnut sowing. Strictly avoid disturbing the soil after Groundnut peg initiation.

## Crop Rotation
Groundnut is an excellent rotation crop because it enriches the soil.
Rotate Groundnut with cereals like wheat, maize, or pearl millet.
Avoid rotating Groundnut with other legumes to prevent the buildup of soil-borne diseases.

## Intercropping
Groundnut is widely used as a profitable intercrop.
Intercrop Groundnut with Pigeon pea (Tur) in a 3:1 or 6:1 ratio.
Groundnut can also be intercropped with cotton or sunflower.

## Major Diseases

### Groundnut Tikka Disease (Early and Late Leaf Spot)
Symptoms: Dark brown to black circular spots surrounded by a yellow halo appear on Groundnut leaves, causing premature defoliation.
Causes: Fungal infection (Cercospora arachidicola and Cercosporidium personatum).
Prevention: Plant resistant Groundnut varieties. Practice crop rotation.
Treatment: Spray Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil when spots first appear on the Groundnut crop.

### Groundnut Collar Rot
Symptoms: Groundnut seedlings wilt suddenly. The stem at the soil line turns brown, rots, and gets covered in black fungal spores.
Causes: Fungal infection (Aspergillus niger).
Prevention: Always treat Groundnut seeds with Trichoderma viride before sowing.
Treatment: Drench the affected Groundnut rows with Carbendazim.

### Groundnut Rust
Symptoms: Small, orange-red powdery pustules appear on the lower surface of Groundnut leaves.
Causes: Fungal infection (Puccinia arachidis).
Prevention: Remove self-sown volunteer Groundnut plants from previous seasons.
Treatment: Spray Hexaconazole or Propiconazole on the infected Groundnut plants.

## Major Pests

### Groundnut White Grub
Symptoms: White, C-shaped grubs live in the soil and eat Groundnut roots and pods, causing the plant to wilt and die in patches.
Prevention: Deep summer ploughing exposes white grubs to predatory birds and the sun.
Treatment: Apply Chlorpyrifos through drenching or mix Phorate granules in the Groundnut soil.

### Groundnut Leaf Miner
Symptoms: Tiny caterpillars mine into the Groundnut leaves, creating transparent zig-zag tunnels. Later, they fold the leaves and feed inside.
Prevention: Set up light traps to catch adult Leaf Miner moths.
Treatment: Spray Quinalphos or Dimethoate on the infested Groundnut crop.

### Groundnut Aphids
Symptoms: Black insects cluster on tender Groundnut shoots, sucking sap and transmitting the Groundnut Rosette Virus.
Prevention: Maintain early and synchronous planting of Groundnut in the region.
Treatment: Spray Imidacloprid if Groundnut aphids are widespread.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Calcium deficiency causes empty Groundnut pods (known as "pops") and blackened plumules inside the seed.
Sulphur deficiency causes young Groundnut leaves to turn pale yellow while older leaves remain green.
Iron deficiency causes interveinal chlorosis (yellowing) in new Groundnut leaves in calcareous soils.

## Harvesting
Harvest Groundnut when the leaves turn yellow and begin to dry.
Pull out a few Groundnut plants; if the inner shell of the pod is dark and the kernel is full, the crop is ready.
Irrigate the Groundnut field lightly before harvesting if the soil is too hard for pulling.

## Post Harvest Management
After uprooting, leave the Groundnut plants in the field for 2-3 days to dry in the sun.
Pluck the Groundnut pods from the vines manually or using a mechanical thresher.
Dry the Groundnut pods thoroughly until the moisture content drops to 8-9%.

## Storage
Store Groundnut in the form of pods (unshelled) rather than bare kernels to prevent pest attacks and rancidity.
Use completely dry, well-ventilated gunny bags for Groundnut storage.
Ensure the Groundnut storage area is completely free of moisture to prevent deadly Aflatoxin contamination.

## Expected Yield
A well-managed irrigated Groundnut crop yields 2 to 3 tonnes of pods per hectare.
Rainfed Groundnut yields average 1 to 1.5 tonnes per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Failing to apply Gypsum results in visually large but completely empty Groundnut pods.
Disturbing the soil (weeding/hoeing) during the Groundnut pegging stage snaps the pegs and destroys the yield.
Storing poorly dried Groundnut pods leads to Aflatoxin fungus, making the crop highly toxic and unsellable.

## Best Farming Practices
Always test Groundnut seed germination percentage before large-scale sowing.
Ensure a loose seedbed so Groundnut pegs can easily enter the soil.
Practice timely weed control during the critical first 45 days of Groundnut growth.

## Government Recommendations
ICAR recommends using polythene mulch in summer Groundnut farming to conserve moisture and increase soil temperature.
Agricultural departments urge farmers to strictly apply Trichoderma seed treatment to combat widespread Groundnut collar rot.

## Interesting Facts
Groundnut is technically not a nut; it is a legume related to beans and lentils.
Groundnut flowers grow above ground, but the heavy flower stalk bends down and pushes the developing pod underground to mature.
Groundnut oil is widely used for cooking because it has a high smoke point and a neutral flavor.
