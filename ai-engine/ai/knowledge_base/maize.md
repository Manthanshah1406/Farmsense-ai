# Maize

## Scientific Name
Zea mays

## Overview
Maize (corn) is a highly versatile crop used for food, animal feed, and industrial products. Maize farming is characterized by its high yield potential. Maize responds extremely well to proper nutrient and water management.

## Suitable Climate
Maize requires a warm climate for optimal growth.
Maize needs an ideal temperature between 21°C and 27°C.
Maize cannot tolerate frost and growth is severely stunted by freezing temperatures.

## Suitable Soil
Maize thrives in deep, well-drained, fertile soils with good organic matter.
Loam and sandy-loam soils are ideal for Maize cultivation.
Maize prefers a soil pH between 6.5 and 7.5.
Maize is highly sensitive to waterlogging.

## Land Preparation
Maize requires a deeply ploughed seedbed to allow strong root penetration.
Plough the Maize field to a depth of 20-25 cm.
Level the Maize field perfectly to prevent any water stagnation.

## Seed Selection
Select high-yielding Maize hybrids suited for the local growing season.
Ensure Maize seeds have a germination rate of at least 90%.
Choose drought-tolerant Maize varieties for rainfed areas.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Maize seeds with Imidacloprid to protect against early shoot fly and termite attacks.
Treat Maize seeds with Thiram or Captan to prevent soil-borne fungal diseases.

## Sowing Time
Kharif Maize is sown in June-July with the onset of the monsoon.
Rabi Maize is sown in October-November in regions with mild winters.
Spring Maize is sown in February-March.

## Spacing
Maintain a row-to-row spacing of 60 cm for Maize.
Maintain a plant-to-plant spacing of 20 cm for Maize.
The standard Maize seed rate is 8 kg to 10 kg per acre.

## Water Requirement
Maize requires 500 mm to 700 mm of water during its lifecycle.
Maize is highly sensitive to both water stress and waterlogging.
Adequate soil moisture is critical during Maize tasseling and silking stages.

## Irrigation Management
Irrigate Maize immediately after sowing if soil moisture is low.
The most critical Maize irrigation stages are tasseling, silking, and grain filling.
Ensure excellent drainage in the Maize field, as standing water for even 24 hours can destroy the crop.

## Nutrient Requirement
Maize is a heavy feeder and requires large amounts of Nitrogen.
Maize requires Phosphorus for strong root establishment.
Maize requires Potassium for stalk strength and disease resistance.

## Recommended Fertilizer
A general Maize fertilizer recommendation is 120 kg Nitrogen, 60 kg Phosphorus, and 40 kg Potassium per hectare.
Apply one-third of Nitrogen and full Phosphorus and Potassium at Maize sowing.
Top-dress the remaining Nitrogen in two splits at the knee-high stage and tasseling stage of Maize.

## Organic Farming Tips
Apply abundant farmyard manure (FYM) to the Maize field one month before sowing.
Use Azospirillum and PSB bio-fertilizers to boost Maize nutrient uptake.
Control Maize pests using Neem oil and Trichogramma egg parasitoids.

## Weed Management
Weeds severely restrict Maize growth during the first 30 to 45 days.
Apply Atrazine as a pre-emergence herbicide within two days of Maize sowing.
Perform manual weeding and earthing-up (mounding soil around the base) at the knee-high stage of Maize.

## Crop Rotation
Maize crop rotation is essential to maintain soil fertility.
Rotate Maize with legumes like soybean, chickpea, or groundnut.
A Maize-Wheat or Maize-Potato rotation is highly profitable.

## Intercropping
Maize is an excellent crop for intercropping due to its tall structure.
Intercrop Maize with moong, urad, or soybean to suppress weeds and fix nitrogen.
Maize and cowpea intercropping is standard for producing high-quality fodder.

## Major Diseases

### Maize Turcicum Leaf Blight
Symptoms: Large, long, elliptical greyish-green or brown lesions appear on Maize leaves.
Causes: Fungal infection (Exserohilum turcicum).
Prevention: Plant resistant Maize hybrids. Practice crop rotation.
Treatment: Spray Mancozeb or Propiconazole when lesions first appear on the Maize crop.

### Maize Maydis Leaf Blight
Symptoms: Small, diamond-shaped, pale green to brown spots appear on Maize leaves.
Causes: Fungal infection (Bipolaris maydis).
Prevention: Destroy Maize crop debris after harvest.
Treatment: Spray Mancozeb on the infected Maize plants.

### Maize Stalk Rot
Symptoms: The lower Maize stalk turns brown, soft, and spongy, leading to the collapse of the Maize plant.
Causes: Fungal/Bacterial infection often triggered by waterlogging and high temperature.
Prevention: Avoid waterlogging in the Maize field. Maintain a balanced Nitrogen-Potassium ratio.
Treatment: Drench the base of the Maize plants with Bleaching powder solution or validamycin.

## Major Pests

### Maize Fall Armyworm (FAW)
Symptoms: Larvae feed aggressively on Maize leaves, creating ragged holes and producing large amounts of sawdust-like droppings in the Maize whorl.
Prevention: Deep ploughing exposes FAW pupae. Plant Maize synchronously in the region.
Treatment: Spray Emamectin Benzoate or Spinetoram specifically into the Maize whorl.

### Maize Stem Borer
Symptoms: Caterpillars tunnel into the Maize stem, causing "dead heart" in young plants and weakening mature stems.
Prevention: Remove and destroy dead Maize stubble.
Treatment: Apply Cartap Hydrochloride granules inside the Maize whorls.

### Maize Aphids
Symptoms: Colonies of tiny insects suck sap from Maize tassels and leaves, covering them with sticky honeydew.
Prevention: Conserve ladybird beetles in the Maize field.
Treatment: Spray Imidacloprid if the Maize aphid infestation is severe.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Nitrogen deficiency causes V-shaped yellowing starting from the tip of the lower Maize leaves.
Phosphorus deficiency causes the edges of young Maize leaves to turn dark green or purple.
Zinc deficiency causes broad bands of bleached white tissue on either side of the Maize leaf midrib (White Bud disease).

## Harvesting
Harvest Maize when the husks turn brown and dry.
Harvest Maize when the grain moisture drops to 20-25%.
Look for the black layer formation at the base of the Maize kernel, indicating physiological maturity.

## Post Harvest Management
Dehusk and dry the Maize cobs in the sun for several days.
Shell the Maize grains once the cob is completely dry.
Clean the Maize grains to remove broken pieces and dust.

## Storage
Dry Maize grains to a moisture content of 10-12% before storage.
Store Maize in well-ventilated, moisture-proof bins.
Use Aluminum Phosphide fumigation to protect stored Maize from weevils and grain borers.

## Expected Yield
A well-managed irrigated Maize hybrid can yield 6 to 8 tonnes per hectare.
Rainfed Maize yields typically range from 2.5 to 4 tonnes per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Allowing water to stagnate in the Maize field destroys the crop within days.
Applying all Nitrogen at the time of sowing leads to leaching; Maize requires split Nitrogen doses.
Ignoring Fall Armyworm at the early stage leads to 100% Maize crop loss.

## Best Farming Practices
Perform earthing-up (piling soil at the base) during the Maize knee-high stage to prevent lodging.
Always apply fertilizers alongside the Maize row, rather than broadcasting.
Use traps and pheromones for early detection of Maize pests.

## Government Recommendations
The government encourages cultivating Maize as an alternative to water-guzzling rice.
ICAR recommends using Bio-fortified Maize (QPM) varieties for better human nutrition.
Follow strict Fall Armyworm management protocols issued by local agricultural extensions.

## Interesting Facts
Maize is the highest yielding cereal crop globally.
Every Maize ear typically has an even number of rows of kernels.
Maize is used to produce ethanol, bioplastics, and thousands of industrial goods.
