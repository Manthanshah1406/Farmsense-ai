# Cotton

## Scientific Name
Gossypium hirsutum / Gossypium herbaceum

## Overview
Cotton is the most important commercial fiber crop globally. Cotton farming requires a long frost-free period, abundant sunshine, and a dry climate during the maturation stage. Cotton is often referred to as "White Gold."

## Suitable Climate
Cotton requires a hot and sunny climate.
Cotton requires a minimum of 200 frost-free days.
The ideal temperature for Cotton growth is between 21°C and 30°C.
Cotton requires dry weather during boll opening and harvesting.

## Suitable Soil
Cotton grows excellently in deep, moisture-retentive black clayey soils (Black Cotton Soil).
Cotton can also be grown in well-drained alluvial and loamy soils.
Cotton requires a soil pH between 6.0 and 8.0.
Cotton is highly sensitive to waterlogging.

## Land Preparation
Deep ploughing is essential for Cotton to allow its taproot to penetrate deeply.
Plough the Cotton field 20-25 cm deep, followed by harrowing to break clods.
Prepare ridges and furrows to facilitate proper irrigation and drainage for the Cotton crop.

## Seed Selection
Select Bt Cotton hybrids to protect the crop from American bollworm.
Choose Cotton varieties resistant to leaf curl virus.
Ensure the Cotton seeds are delinted (free from short fuzz) for uniform germination.

## Seed Treatment
Treat Cotton seeds with Imidacloprid to protect young seedlings from sucking pests like aphids and jassids.
Treat Cotton seeds with Trichoderma viride to prevent soil-borne diseases.

## Sowing Time
In irrigated areas, Cotton is sown in April-May.
In rainfed areas, Cotton is sown with the onset of the monsoon in June-July.
Late sowing of Cotton leads to heavy pest attacks and reduced yields.

## Spacing
For irrigated Bt Cotton, maintain a spacing of 120 cm by 60 cm or 90 cm by 90 cm.
For rainfed Cotton, a closer spacing of 90 cm by 45 cm is used.
The standard Cotton seed rate is 1.5 kg to 2 kg per acre for hybrids.

## Water Requirement
Cotton requires 500 mm to 700 mm of water throughout its lifecycle.
Cotton requires adequate moisture during the vegetative and flowering stages.
Cotton requires absolutely dry conditions during the boll bursting stage.

## Irrigation Management
Provide the first irrigation to Cotton 20-30 days after sowing.
The most critical Cotton irrigation stages are square formation, flowering, and boll development.
Stop irrigating Cotton when 30-40% of the bolls have opened to prevent fiber damage.

## Nutrient Requirement
Cotton requires Nitrogen for stem and leaf growth.
Cotton requires Phosphorus for early root growth and boll setting.
Cotton requires Potassium to improve fiber strength and quality.
Cotton requires Magnesium and Boron for proper boll retention.

## Recommended Fertilizer
A general fertilizer recommendation for irrigated Bt Cotton is 120 kg Nitrogen, 60 kg Phosphorus, and 60 kg Potassium per hectare.
Apply Nitrogen in three split doses (at sowing, square formation, and peak flowering) to the Cotton crop.
Foliar spray of 2% DAP and 1% Potassium Chloride during Cotton flowering improves boll weight.

## Organic Farming Tips
Use farmyard manure and castor cake extensively during Cotton land preparation.
Spray Neem Seed Kernel Extract (NSKE) 5% to repel pests from the Cotton crop.
Use pheromone traps and light traps to manage Cotton bollworms organically.

## Weed Management
Cotton growth is very slow in the first 45 days, making it highly vulnerable to weeds.
Apply pre-emergence Pendimethalin within 48 hours of Cotton sowing.
Perform manual weeding or use a tractor-drawn cultivator between Cotton rows 30 and 45 days after sowing.

## Crop Rotation
Rotating Cotton breaks the life cycle of dangerous pests and diseases.
Cotton-Wheat and Cotton-Sorghum are popular rotations.
Rotate Cotton with legumes like groundnut or soybean to restore soil nitrogen.

## Intercropping
Intercropping maximizes land use in widely spaced Cotton fields.
Intercrop Cotton with green gram (moong), black gram (urad), or soybean.
Planting a few rows of Marigold around the Cotton field acts as a trap crop for nematodes and bollworms.

## Major Diseases

### Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV)
Symptoms: Cotton leaves curl upwards or downwards, veins thicken, and leaf-like structures (enations) grow on the underside of leaves.
Causes: Viral infection transmitted by Whiteflies.
Prevention: Plant resistant Cotton varieties. Eradicate alternative weed hosts.
Treatment: Viruses have no cure; control the Whitefly vector by spraying Diafenthiuron or Flonicamid on the Cotton crop.

### Cotton Bacterial Blight (Angular Leaf Spot)
Symptoms: Angular, water-soaked, dark brown spots appear on Cotton leaves, restricted by leaf veins.
Causes: Bacterial infection (Xanthomonas campestris).
Prevention: Use acid-delinted Cotton seeds.
Treatment: Spray Copper Oxychloride mixed with Streptocycline on the infected Cotton plants.

### Cotton Fusarium Wilt
Symptoms: Cotton leaves turn yellow, droop, and the entire plant wilts. Cutting the Cotton stem reveals brown vascular discoloration.
Causes: Fungal infection (Fusarium oxysporum) in the soil.
Prevention: Practice long crop rotations. Avoid planting Cotton in heavily infested soils.
Treatment: Drench the soil around the Cotton roots with Carbendazim.

## Major Pests

### Cotton Pink Bollworm
Symptoms: Larvae bore into Cotton bolls, eat the seeds, and damage the developing lint. Infested bolls fail to open properly.
Prevention: Destroy all Cotton crop residue immediately after harvest. Strictly observe a closed season (no Cotton in the field for 3-4 months).
Treatment: Install pheromone traps. Spray Quinalphos or Thiodicarb when Pink Bollworm crosses the economic threshold in the Cotton field.

### Cotton Whitefly
Symptoms: Tiny white insects suck sap from the underside of Cotton leaves and excrete sticky honeydew, causing black sooty mold and transmitting leaf curl virus.
Prevention: Avoid excessive Nitrogen application which makes Cotton leaves succulent and attractive to whiteflies.
Treatment: Spray Spiromesifen, Pyriproxyfen, or Neem oil on the Cotton crop.

### Cotton Jassids (Leafhoppers)
Symptoms: Jassids suck sap from Cotton leaves, causing the leaf margins to turn yellow, then red, and curl downwards (hopper burn).
Prevention: Plant Cotton varieties with hairy leaves that deter jassids.
Treatment: Spray Imidacloprid or Dinotefuran on the Cotton plants.

## Nutrient Deficiency
Nitrogen deficiency causes generalized yellowing and stunted Cotton growth.
Magnesium deficiency causes Cotton leaf reddening (Red Leaf Disease) where the leaf tissue turns purplish-red but veins remain green.
Boron deficiency causes excessive shedding of Cotton squares and bolls.

## Harvesting
Cotton harvesting (picking) is done manually in multiple stages as bolls open.
Begin Cotton picking when the bolls are fully open and dry.
Avoid picking Cotton when there is dew or moisture on the lint.

## Post Harvest Management
Dry the picked Cotton seed-cotton in the sun for a few days to reduce moisture.
Store clean Cotton separately from stained or pest-damaged Cotton.
Transport the Cotton to gins to separate the lint from the seeds.

## Storage
Store Cotton in a dry, fire-proof godown.
Never store Cotton on bare earth; always use wooden pallets.
Keep the Cotton storage area free from dust, oil, and moisture to preserve fiber quality.

## Expected Yield
Irrigated Bt Cotton can yield 2.5 to 3.5 tonnes of seed cotton (kapas) per hectare.
Rainfed Cotton yields are much lower, averaging 1 to 1.5 tonnes per hectare.

## Common Farmer Mistakes
Applying pesticides calendars-based rather than scouting for pests destroys natural enemies in the Cotton field.
Over-irrigating Cotton leads to excessive vegetative growth and heavy shedding of bolls.
Leaving Cotton stalks in the field after harvest provides a breeding ground for the Pink Bollworm.

## Best Farming Practices
Adopt Integrated Pest Management (IPM) to control Cotton pests sustainably.
Nip the terminal growing point of the Cotton plant at 90 days to stop vertical growth and redirect energy to boll development.
Install bird perches in the Cotton field to encourage predatory birds that eat insect pests.

## Government Recommendations
The government strongly advises terminating the Cotton crop by January to break the Pink Bollworm cycle.
ICAR promotes High-Density Planting System (HDPS) with specific compact Cotton varieties for higher yields.
Use strictly approved and certified Bt Cotton seeds to avoid spurious seeds.

## Interesting Facts
Cotton is both a fiber crop and a food crop (cottonseed oil is used for cooking).
Bt Cotton is genetically modified to produce a protein toxic to the American bollworm, drastically reducing pesticide use.
India is one of the largest producers and consumers of Cotton in the world.
